# Open Logic translations

This is the catalogue and production standard for independent translations and accessibility work based on the [Open Logic Project](https://openlogicproject.org/). The editions listed here are adaptations; listing does not imply endorsement by the Open Logic Project.

**[Choose a language in the visual edition hub](https://kokunoyumeto.github.io/OpenLogic-translations/)** · [Open mathematics programme (Indonesian / English)](https://kokunoyumeto.github.io/program-matematika-indonesia/)

The [frozen English source snapshot](https://github.com/OpenLogicProject/OpenLogic/tree/9620cc73f9c8e0ad003c514a5d3748f29611c4c0) contains 722 tracked content `.tex` files. Technical revision identities are recorded in the catalogue. Coverage of those files and coverage of a reading PDF are reported separately. In particular, “722 translated sources” does **not** mean that all 722 are present in one reader.

## Accessibility infrastructure

The accessible Open Logic book is infrastructure, not another language edition. Its latest accepted checkpoint integrates 50 of 80 planned source tranches (62.5%). Later tranches have prepared or authored material but have not all passed cumulative acceptance. No public repository is claimed here yet. Across editions, semantic responsive HTML, reflowable EPUB, native MathML, image descriptions, offline packaging, and applicable low-bitrate media are publication baselines; missing modes remain explicit rather than being hidden behind a PDF claim.

A **non-academic-register English** lane (initially proposed as “Plain-Language English” and “accessible-register English”) is now assigned to the established accessibility owner as a distinct aligned register edition. Its sociolinguistic premise is that familiarity with university-register language is unevenly inherited through family and schooling; lacking that inherited linguistic capital says nothing about a reader's intellectual capacity. The lane therefore translates the complete information into an evidenced non-academic adult register while presuming the reader is fully capable of the mathematics. Every definition, quantifier, logical-scope relation, notation item, cross-reference, argument step and proof obligation must remain: this is translation, not abridgment, condescension, semantic simplification, prerequisite insertion or “dumbing down.” The edition proceeds on that sufficiently plausible access thesis: research improves target-register choices, detects exclusion and measures results, but does not impose a go/no-go gate. Its evidence base must include linguistic capital and social reproduction, critical EAP/EMI, World Englishes, raciolinguistics, linguistic imperialism, decolonial and multilingual education, alongside intralingual translation and adult plain/controlled-language research. The research must identify specified adult target registers—for example regionally evidenced ordinary English and English-as-a-lingua-franca conventions—rather than invent one universal Plain English. OpenLogic remains part of the broader mathematics programme: when a prerequisite is genuinely absent, the edition points to the appropriate prerequisite work instead of adding analogies or quietly rewriting the book into a remedial course. No translated unit, completed pilot, or public release is claimed yet; the exact [scope record](evidence/PLAIN_LANGUAGE_ENGLISH_SCOPE_20260905.json) keeps that boundary explicit.

## Active research editions

- **Paired Dutch registers:** one complete standard scholarly Dutch edition and one complete adult **gewone-mensentaal** Dutch register translation have been commissioned from the same 722-unit source and segment spine. The pair is intended to permit direct within-language comparison: information, mathematics and proof obligations remain identical while academic-register choices change. The ordinary-register edition is not abridged, remedial or child-directed. The dedicated owner task is still completing worktree setup, so zero translated units and no repository, reader, release or DOI are claimed yet; see the [commission record](evidence/DUTCH_DUAL_REGISTER_COMMISSION_20260906.json).
- **Inter-Farsi OpenLogic:** active corpus-wide scaffold/edition work. Its design separates a controlled pluricentric register from Iranian Persian and keeps Perso-Arabic and Tajik Cyrillic realization, regional adaptation, and notation profiles as typed layers. It is not yet listed as a public complete translation.
- **Romance interlanguage:** 722 provisional baseline files exist, but zero units were canon-admitted at the last exact checkpoint. It is research scaffolding, not a complete edition.
- **Latin:** planned as a complete `la-Latn` Neo-Latin edition plus a separate bilingual learner companion for mathematically minded Latin readers. Historical mathematical and logical Latin, modern Latinity guidance, and an explicit neologism ledger will govern the prose; Peano's *Latino sine flexione* is comparative evidence, not the target register. No translated unit or owner is claimed yet.

## What “done” means here

A new complete edition must translate all 722 tracked source files **and** provide one coherent standalone reader containing the complete declared closure. It must bind every segment to the language’s actual scholarly canon, preserve formulas and identifiers, pass script-aware structural and visual QA, publish editable sources and evidence, and verify released bytes anonymously. See [`TRANSLATION_STANDARD.md`](TRANSLATION_STANDARD.md).

The next-language shortlist and its limitations are in [`PRIORITIES.md`](PRIORITIES.md). The frozen v3 order is a historical planning input under which lanes were commissioned; the current allocation study does not establish a live global Top 10. Its actionable production constraints are recorded in the [adjacent-evidence audit](evidence/ADJACENT_COMPUTE_ACTIONABLE_FINDINGS_20260905.json).

## Recovered mathematics translation work

The six book-based recovered-package assignments are now delivered publicly: see the [bounded package directory](RELATED_MATHEMATICS.md#completed-bounded-packages). This is completion of the recovery packages, not of every textbook in every language.
