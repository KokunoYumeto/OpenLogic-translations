  11. \olsection{Introduction}
  13. Giving the meaning of expressions is the domain of semantics. The
  14. central concept in semantics is that of satisfaction in
  15. !!a{structure}. !!^a{structure} gives meaning to the building blocks
  16. of the language: !!a{domain} is a non-empty set of objects. The
  17. quantifiers are interpreted as ranging over this domain, !!{constant}s
  18. are assigned elements in the domain, !!{function}s are assigned
  19. functions from the !!{domain} to itself, and !!{predicate}s are
  20. assigned relations on the !!{domain}. The !!{domain} together with
  21. assignments to the basic vocabulary constitutes !!a{structure}.
  22. !!^{variable}s may appear in !!{formula}s, and in order to give a
  23. semantics, we also have to assign !!{element}s of the !!{domain} to
  24. them---this is a variable assignment. The satisfaction relation,
  25. finally, brings these together. !!^a{formula} may be satisfied in
  26. !!a{structure}~$\Struct{M}$ relative to !!a{variable} assignment~$s$,
  27. written as $\Sat{M}{!A}[s]$. This relation is also defined by
  28. induction on the structure of~$!A$, using the truth tables for the
  29. logical connectives to define, say, satisfaction of $(!A \land !B)$ in
  30. terms of satisfaction (or not) of $!A$ and ~$!B$. It then turns out
  31. that the !!{variable} assignment is irrelevant if the !!{formula}~$!A$
  32. is !!a{sentence}, i.e., has no free variables, and so we can talk of
  33. !!{sentence}s being simply satisfied (or not) in !!{structure}s.
  35. On the basis of the satisfaction relation $\Sat{M}{!A}$ for !!{sentence}s
  36. we can then define the basic semantic notions of validity, entailment,
  37. and satisfiability. !!^a{sentence} is valid, $\Entails !A$, if every
  38. !!{structure} satisfies it. It is entailed by a set of !!{sentence}s,
  39. $\Gamma \Entails !A$, if every !!{structure} that satisfies all the
  40. !!{sentence}s in~$\Gamma$ also satisfies~$!A$. And a set of !!{sentence}s
  41. is satisfiable if some !!{structure} satisfies all !!{sentence}s in it
  42. at the same time. Because !!{formula}s are inductively defined, and
  43. satisfaction is in turn defined by induction on the structure of
  44. !!{formula}s, we can use induction to prove properties of our
  45. semantics and to relate the semantic notions defined.

  16. !!{formula}s, on the one hand, and !!{structure}s on the other, are

  19. !!a{structure}---if the term is just a constant, its !!{value} is the
  20. object assigned to the constant by the !!{structure}, and if it is
  21. built up using !!{function}s, the !!{value} is computed from the
  22. !!{value}s of constants and the functions assigned to the functions in
  23. the term. !!^a{formula} is \emph{satisfied} in !!a{structure} if the
  24. interpretation given to the predicates makes the !!{formula} true in
  25. the domain of the !!{structure}. This notion of satisfaction is
  26. specified inductively: the specification of the !!{structure} directly
  27. states when atomic !!{formula}s are satisfied, and we define when a
  28. complex !!{formula} is satisfied depending on the main connective or
  29. quantifier and whether or not the immediate !!{subformula}s are
  30. satisfied.

  33. immediate !!{subformula} of a quantified !!{formula} has a free
  34. !!{variable}, and !!{structure}s don't specify the !!{value}s of
  35. !!{variable}s. In order to deal with this difficulty, we also
  36. introduce \emph{variable assignments} and define satisfaction not with
  37. respect to !!a{structure} alone, but with respect to !!a{structure}
  38. plus !!a{variable} assignment.

  39. for some !!{structure}~$\Struct M$. If $\Gamma$ is not satisfiable it is
  40. called \emph{unsatisfiable}.
  41. \end{defn}
  43. \begin{prop}
  44. A sentence $!A$ is valid iff $\Gamma \Entails !A$ for every set of
  45. sentences~$\Gamma$.
  46. \end{prop}
  48. \begin{proof}
  49. For the forward direction, let $!A$ be valid, and let $\Gamma$ be a
  50. set of sentences. Let $\Struct M$ be !!a{structure} so that
  51. $\Sat{M}{\Gamma}$. Since $!A$ is valid, $\Sat{M}{!A}$, hence $\Gamma
  52. \Entails !A$.
  54. For the contrapositive of the reverse direction, let $!A$ be invalid,
  55. so there is !!a{structure}~$\Struct M$ with $\Sat/{M}{!A}$. When $\Gamma
  56. = \{ \ltrue \}$, since $\ltrue$ is valid, $\Sat{M}{\Gamma}$. Hence,
  57. there is !!a{structure}~$\Struct M$ so that $\Sat{M}{\Gamma}$ but
  58. $\Sat/{M}{!A}$, hence $\Gamma$ does not entail $!A$.
  59. \end{proof}
  61. \begin{prop}
  62. \ollabel{prop:entails-unsat}
  63. $\Gamma \Entails !A$ iff $\Gamma \cup \{\lnot !A\}$ is unsatisfiable.
  64. \end{prop}
  66. \begin{proof}
  67. For the forward direction, suppose $\Gamma \Entails !A$ and suppose to the
  68. contrary that there is !!a{structure}~$\Struct M$ so that $\Sat{M}{\Gamma
  69. \cup \{ \lnot !A \}}$. Since $\Sat{M}{\Gamma}$ and $\Gamma \Entails
  70. !A$, $\Sat{M}{!A}$. Also, since $\Sat{M}{\Gamma\cup \{ \lnot !A \}}$,
  71. $\Sat{M}{\lnot !A}$, so we have both $\Sat{M}{!A}$ and $\Sat/{M}{!A}$,
  72. a contradiction. Hence, there can be no such !!{structure}~$\Struct M$, so
  73. $\Gamma \cup \{ \lnot !A \}$ is unsatisfiable.
  75. For the reverse direction, suppose $\Gamma \cup \{ \lnot !A \}$ is
  76. unsatisfiable. So for every !!{structure}~$\Struct M$, either
  77. $\Sat/{M}{\Gamma}$ or $\Sat{M}{!A}$. Hence, for every !!{structure}
  78. $\Struct M$ with $\Sat{M}{\Gamma}$, $\Sat{M}{!A}$, so $\Gamma \Entails
  79. !A$.
  80. \end{proof}
  82. \begin{prob}
  83. \begin{enumerate}
  84. \item Show that $\Gamma \Entails \bot$ iff $\Gamma$ is unsatisfiable.
  85. \item Show that $\Gamma \cup \{!A\} \Entails \bot$ iff $\Gamma \Entails \lnot !A$.
  86. \item Suppose $c$ does not occur in $!A$ or $\Gamma$. Show that
  87. $\Gamma \Entails \lforall[x][!A]$ iff $\Gamma \Entails
  88. \Subst{!A}{c}{x}$.
  89. \end{enumerate}
  90. \end{prob}
  92. \begin{prop}
  93. If $\Gamma \subseteq \Gamma'$ and $\Gamma \Entails !A$, then $\Gamma'
  94. \Entails !A$.
  95. \end{prop}
  97. \begin{proof}
  98. Suppose that $\Gamma \subseteq \Gamma'$ and $\Gamma \Entails !A$. Let
  99. $\Struct M$ be a structure such that $\Sat{M}{\Gamma'}$; then $\Sat{M}{\Gamma}$,
  100. and since $\Gamma \Entails !A$, we get that $\Sat{M}{!A}$. Hence,
  101. whenever $\Sat{M}{\Gamma'}$, $\Sat{M}{!A}$, so $\Gamma' \Entails !A$.
  102. \end{proof}
  105. \begin{thm}[Semantic Deduction Theorem]
  106. \ollabel{thm:sem-deduction}
  107. $\Gamma \cup \{!A\} \Entails !B$ iff $\Gamma \Entails !A \lif !B$.
  108. \end{thm}
  110. \begin{proof}
  111. For the forward direction, let $\Gamma \cup \{ !A \} \Entails !B$ and
  112. let $\Struct M$ be !!a{structure} so that $\Sat{M}{\Gamma}$. If
  113. $\Sat{M}{!A}$, then $\Sat{M}{\Gamma \cup \{ !A \} }$, so since $\Gamma
  114. \cup \{ !A \}$ entails $!B$, we get $\Sat{M}{!B}$. Therefore,
  115. $\Sat{M}{!A \lif !B}$, so $\Gamma \Entails !A \lif !B$.
  117. For the reverse direction, let $\Gamma \Entails !A \lif !B$ and
  118. $\Struct M$ be !!a{structure} so that $\Sat{M}{\Gamma \cup \{ !A
  119. \}}$. Then $\Sat{M}{\Gamma}$, so $\Sat{M}{!A \lif !B}$, and since
  120. $\Sat{M}{!A}$, $\Sat{M}{!B}$. Hence, whenever $\Sat{M}{\Gamma \cup \{
  121. !A \} }$, $\Sat{M}{!B}$, so $\Gamma \cup \{ !A \} \Entails !B$.
  122. \end{proof}
