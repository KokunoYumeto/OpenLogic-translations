Section. Rules and derivation. For the following, let first Gamma, then capital Delta, then capital Pi, and finally capital Lambda represent finite sequences of sentences. Definition. Title: Sequent. A sequent is an expression of the form antecedent containing Gamma; sequent arrow; succedent containing capital Delta where Gamma and capital Delta are finite (possibly empty) sequences of sentences of the language L . Gamma is called the antecedent, while capital Delta is the succedent. Explanation. The intuitive idea behind a sequent is: if all of the sentences in the antecedent hold, then at least one of the sentences in the succedent holds. That is, if Gamma equals the finite sequence first formula A sub one, continuing through the omitted intermediate entries, and finally formula A sub m and capital Delta equals the finite sequence first formula B sub one, continuing through the omitted intermediate entries, and finally formula B sub n , then antecedent containing Gamma; sequent arrow; succedent containing capital Delta holds iff the conditional whose antecedent is open parenthesis, the conjunction beginning with formula A sub one, continuing through the omitted intermediate conjunctions, and ending with formula A sub m, close parenthesis; and whose consequent is open parenthesis, the disjunction beginning with formula B sub one, continuing through the omitted intermediate disjunctions, and ending with formula B sub n, close parenthesis holds. There are two special cases: where Gamma is empty and when capital Delta is empty. When Gamma is empty, i.e., m equals zero , antecedent containing no formulas; sequent arrow; succedent containing capital Delta holds iff the disjunction beginning with formula B sub one, continuing through the omitted intermediate disjunctions, and ending with formula B sub n holds. When capital Delta is empty, i.e., n equals zero , antecedent containing Gamma; sequent arrow; succedent containing no formulas holds iff the negation of open parenthesis, the conjunction beginning with formula A sub one, continuing through the omitted intermediate conjunctions, and ending with formula A sub m, close parenthesis does. We say a sequent is valid iff the corresponding sentence is valid. If Gamma is a sequence of sentences, we write first Gamma, then formula A for the result of appending formula A to the right end of Gamma (and first formula A, then Gamma for the result of appending formula A to the left end of Gamma ). If capital Delta is a sequence of sentences also, then first Gamma, then capital Delta is the concatenation of the two sequences. Definition. Title: Initial Sequent.
