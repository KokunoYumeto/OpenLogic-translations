1% part: intuitionistic-logic2% chapter: introduction3% section: constructive-reasoning45\documentclass[../../../include/open-logic-chapter]{subfiles}67\begin{document}89\olfileid{int}{int}{cr}1011\section{Constructive Reasoning}1213In contrast to extensions of classical logic by modal operators or14second-order quantifiers, intuitionistic logic is ``non-classical'' in15that it restricts classical logic.  Classical logic is16\emph{non-constructive} in various ways. Intuitionistic logic is17intended to capture a more ``constructive'' kind of reasoning18characteristic of a kind of constructive mathematics. The following19examples may serve to illustrate some of the underlying motivations.2021Suppose someone claimed that they had determined a natural number~$n$22with the property that if $n$ is even, the Riemann hypothesis is23true, and if $n$ is odd, the Riemann hypothesis is false. Great24news!{} Whether the Riemann hypothesis is true or not is one of the25big open questions of mathematics, and they seem to have reduced the26problem to one of calculation, that is, to the determination of27whether a specific number is even or not.2829What is the magic value of~$n$? They describe it as follows: $n$ is30the natural number that is equal to $2$ if the Riemann hypothesis is31true, and $3$ otherwise.3233Angrily, you demand your money back. From a classical point of view,34the description above does in fact determine a unique value of $n$;35but what you really want is a value of $n$ that is given36\emph{explicitly}.3738To take another, perhaps less contrived example, consider the39following question. We know that it is possible to raise an irrational40number to a rational power, and get a rational result. For example,41$\sqrt{2}^2 = 2$. What is less clear is whether or not it is possible42to raise an irrational number to an \emph{irrational} power, and get a43rational result. The following theorem answers this in the44affirmative:4546\begin{thm}47There are irrational numbers $a$ and $b$ such that $a^b$ is rational.48\end{thm}4950\begin{proof}51Consider $\sqrt{2}^{\sqrt{2}}$. If this is rational, we are done:52we can let $a = b = \sqrt{2}$. Otherwise, it is irrational. Then we53have54\[55(\sqrt{2}^{\sqrt{2}})^{\sqrt{2}} = \sqrt{2}^{\sqrt{2} \cdot56  \sqrt{2}} = \sqrt{2}^2 = 2,57\]58which is rational. So, in this case, let $a$ be59$\sqrt{2}^{\sqrt{2}}$, and let $b$ be~$\sqrt 2$.60\end{proof}6162Does this constitute a valid proof? Most mathematicians feel that it63does. But again, there is something a little bit unsatisfying here: we64have proved the existence of a pair of real numbers with a certain65property, without being able to say \emph{which} pair of numbers it66is.  It is possible to prove the same result, but in such a way that67the pair $a$, $b$ \emph{is} given in the proof: take $a = \sqrt{3}$68and $b = \log_3 4$. Then69\[70a^b = \sqrt{3}^{\log_3 4} = 3^{1/2 \cdot \log_3 4} = (3^{\log_371  4})^{1/2} = 4^{1/2}= 2,72\]73since $3^{\log_3 x} = x$.7475Intuitionistic logic is designed to capture a kind of reasoning where76moves like the one in the first proof are disallowed. Proving the77existence of an $x$ satisfying~$!A(x)$ means that you have to give a78specific~$x$, and a proof that it satisfies $!A$, like in the second79proof. Proving that $!A$ or $!B$ holds requires that you can prove one80or the other.8182Formally speaking, intuitionistic logic is what you get if83you restrict !!a{derivation} system for classical logic in a certain84way. From the mathematical point of view, these are85just formal deductive systems, but, as already noted, they are86intended to capture a kind of mathematical reasoning. One can take this87to be the kind of reasoning that is justified on a certain88philosophical view of mathematics (such as Brouwer's intuitionism);89one can take it to be a kind of mathematical reasoning which is more90``concrete'' and satisfying (along the lines of Bishop's91constructivism); and one can argue about whether or not the formal92description captures the informal motivation. But whatever93philosophical positions we may hold, we can study intuitionistic logic94as a formally presented logic; and for whatever reasons, many95mathematical logicians find it interesting to do so.9697\end{document}

In classical propositional logic it is possible15to define connectives by others, e.g., one can define $!A \lif !B$ by16$\lnot !A \lor !B$, or $!A \lor !B$ by $\lnot(\lnot !A \land \lnot17!B)$. Thus, presentations of classical logic often introduce some18connectives as abbreviations for these definitions. This is not so in19intuitionistic logic, with two exceptions: $\lnot !A$ can be---and20often is---defined as an abbreviation for $!A \lif \lfalse$. Then, of21course, $\lfalse$ must not itself be defined!{} Also, $!A \liff !B$22can be defined, as in classical logic, as $(!A \lif !B) \land (!B \lif23!A)$.2425!!^{formula}s of propositional intuitionistic logic are built up from26\emph{!!{propositional variable}s} and the propositional27constant~$\lfalse$ using \emph{logical connectives}. We have:2829\begin{enumerate}30\item !!^a{denumerable} set~$\PVar$ of !!{propositional variable}s $\Obj p_0$,31  $\Obj p_1$, \dots32  \item The propositional constant for !!{falsity}~$\lfalse$.33\item The logical connectives: $\land$34  (conjunction), $\lor$ (disjunction), $\lif$ (conditional)35\item Punctuation marks: (, ), and the comma.36\end{enumerate}3738\begin{defn}[Formula]39\ollabel{defn:formulas} The set~$\Frm[L_0]$ of \emph{!!{formula}s} of40propositional intuitionistic logic is defined inductively as follows:41\begin{enumerate}42\item $\lfalse$ is an atomic !!{formula}.4344\item Every !!{propositional variable}~$\Obj p_i$ is an atomic45  !!{formula}.4647\item If $!A$ and $!B$ are !!{formula}s, then $(!A \land48  !B)$ is !!a{formula}.4950\item If $!A$ and $!B$ are !!{formula}s, then $(!A \lor !B)$51  is !!a{formula}.5253\item If $!A$ and $!B$ are !!{formula}s, then $(!A \lif !B)$54  is !!a{formula}.5556\tagitem{limitClause}{Nothing else is !!a{formula}.}{}57\end{enumerate}58\end{defn}5960In addition to the primitive connectives introduced above, we also use61the following \emph{defined} symbols: $\lnot$ (negation) and $\liff$62(!!{biconditional}).  Formulas constructed using the defined operators63are to be understood as follows:64\begin{enumerate}65\item $\lnot !A$ abbreviates $!A \lif \lfalse$.6667\item $!A \liff !B$ abbreviates $(!A \lif !B) \land (!B68  \lif !A)$.69\end{enumerate}7071Although $\lnot$ is officially treated as an abbreviation, we will72sometimes give explicit rules and clauses in definitions for~$\lnot$73as if it were primitive. This is mostly so we can state practice74problems.7576\end{document}

It uses the notion of a ``construction,'' which you21may think of as a constructive proof. (We don't use ``proof'' in the22BHK interpretation so as not to get confused with the notion of23!!a{derivation} in a formal !!{derivation} system.) Based on this intuitive24notion, the BHK interpretation explains the meanings of the25intuitionistic connectives.2627\begin{enumerate}28\item We assume that we know what constitutes a construction of an atomic29  statement.30\item A construction of $!A_1 \land !A_2$ is a pair $\tuple{M_1, M_2}$31  where $M_1$ is a construction of~$!A_1$ and $M_2$ is a construction32  of~$!A_2$.33\item A construction of $!A_1 \lor !A_2$ is a pair $\tuple{s, M}$34  where $s$ is~$1$ and $M$ is a construction of~$!A_1$, or $s$ is~$2$35  and $M$ is a construction of~$!A_2$.36\item A construction of $!A \lif !B$ is a function that converts a37  construction of~$!A$ into a construction of~$!B$.38\item There is no construction for~$\lfalse$ (absurdity).39\item $\lnot !A$ is defined as synonym for $!A \lif \lfalse$. That is,40  a construction of $\lnot !A$ is a function converting a construction41  of~$!A$ into a construction of~$\lfalse$.42\end{enumerate}4344\begin{ex}45Take $\lnot \lfalse$ for example. A construction of it is a function46which, given any construction of $\lfalse$ as input, provides a47construction of~$\lfalse$ as output. Obviously, the identity48function~$\Id{}$ is such a construction: given a construction~$M$49of~$\lfalse$, $\Id{}(M) = M$ yields a construction of~$\lfalse$.50\end{ex}5152Generally speaking, $\lnot !A$ means ``A construction of $!A$ is impossible''.5354\begin{ex}55Let us prove $!A \lif \lnot \lnot !A$ for any proposition~$!A$, which56is $!A \lif ((!A \lif \lfalse) \lif \lfalse)$. Here is59how~$f$ constructs the construction of $(!A \lif \lfalse) \lif60\lfalse$: We have to define a function $g$ which, when given a61construction~$h$ of $!A \lif \lfalse$ as input, outputs a construction62of~$\lfalse$. We can define $g$ as follows: apply the input~$h$ to the63construction~$M$ of $!A$ (that we received earlier). Since the64output~$h(M)$ of $h$ is a construction of $\lfalse$, $f(M)(h) = h(M)$ is a65construction of~$\lfalse$ if $M$ is a construction of~$!A$.66\end{ex}6768\begin{ex}69Let us give a construction for $\lnot(!A \land \lnot !A)$, i.e., $(!A70\land (!A \lif \lfalse)) \lif \lfalse$. That82yields a construction of~$!A$. Then it applies $p_2$ to~$M$, yielding83a construction of~$!A \lif \lfalse$. Such a construction, in turn, is84a function~$p_2(M)$ which, if given as input a construction of~$!A$,85yields a construction of~$\lfalse$. In other words, if we apply86$p_2(M)$ to $p_1(M)$, we get a construction of~$\lfalse$.  Thus, we87can define $f(M) = p_2(M)(p_1(M))$.88\end{ex}8990\begin{ex}91Let us give a construction of $((!A \land !B) \lif !C) \lif (!A \lif92(!B \lif !C))$, i.e., a function $f$ which turns a construction~$g$ of93$(!A \land !B) \lif !C$ into a construction of $(!A \lif (!B \lif94!C))$. Whew!{}111\end{ex}112113The statement $!A \lor \lnot !A$ is called the Law of Excluded114Middle. We can prove it for some specific $!A$ (e.g., $\lfalse \lor115\lnot \lfalse$), but not in general. And let $h_2$137be the function mapping a construction~$M_2$ of $\lnot !A$ to a138construction of $!A \lor \lnot !A$: it maps $M_2$ to $\tuple{2, M_2}$.139140Let $k$ be $\comp{h_1}{g}$: it is a function which, if given a141construction of~$!A$, returns a construction of~$\lfalse$, i.e., it is142a construction of~$!A \lif \lfalse$ or $\lnot !A$. Luckily, there are better !!{derivation} systems for157intuitionistic logic, and more precise semantic interpretations.158\end{document}
