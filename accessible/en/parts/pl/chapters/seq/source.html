  13. \olsection{Rules and \usetoken{P}{derivation}}
  15. For the following, let $\Gamma, \Delta, \Pi, \Lambda$ represent finite
  16. sequences of !!{sentence}s.
  18. \begin{defn}[Sequent]
  19. A \emph{sequent} is an expression of the form
  20. \[
  21. \Gamma \Sequent \Delta
  22. \]
  23. where $\Gamma$ and $\Delta$ are finite (possibly empty) sequences of
  24. !!{sentence}s of the language $\Lang L$. $\Gamma$ is called the
  25. \emph{antecedent}, while $\Delta$ is the \emph{succedent}.
  26. \end{defn}
  28. \begin{explain}
  29. The intuitive idea behind a sequent is: if all of the !!{sentence}s in
  30. the antecedent hold, then at least one of the !!{sentence}s in the
  31. succedent holds. That is, if $\Gamma = \tuple{!A_1, \dots, !A_m}$ and
  32. $\Delta = \tuple{!B_1, \dots, !B_n}$, then $\Gamma \Sequent \Delta$
  33. holds iff
  34. \[
  35. (!A_1 \land \cdots \land !A_m) \lif (!B_1 \lor \cdots \lor
  36. !B_n)
  37. \]
  38. holds. There are two special cases: where $\Gamma$ is empty and when
  39. $\Delta$~is empty. When $\Gamma$ is empty, i.e., $m = 0$, $\quad
  40. \Sequent \Delta$ holds iff $!B_1 \lor \dots \lor !B_n$ holds. When
  41. $\Delta$ is empty, i.e., $n = 0$, $\Gamma \Sequent \quad$ holds iff
  42. $\lnot(!A_1 \land \dots \land !A_m)$ does. We say a sequent is valid
  43. iff the corresponding !!{sentence} is valid.
  44. \end{explain}
  46. If $\Gamma$ is a sequence of !!{sentence}s, we write $\Gamma, !A$ for
  47. the result of appending $!A$ to the right end of~$\Gamma$ (and $!A,
  48. \Gamma$ for the result of appending $!A$ to the left end
  49. of~$\Gamma$). If $\Delta$ is a sequence of !!{sentence}s also, then $\Gamma,
  50. \Delta$ is the concatenation of the two sequences.
  52. \begin{defn}[Initial Sequent]
  53. An \emph{initial sequent} is a sequent
  54. \iftag{prvFalse,prvTrue}{of one of the following forms:
  55. \begin{enumerate}
  56. \item $!A \Sequent !A$
  57. \tagitem{prvTrue}{$\quad \Sequent \ltrue$}{}
  58. \tagitem{prvFalse}{$\lfalse \Sequent \quad$}{}
  59. \end{enumerate}}
  60. {of the form $!A \Sequent !A$} for any !!{sentence} $!A$ in the language.
  61. \end{defn}
  63. !!^{derivation}s in the sequent calculus are certain trees of
  64. sequents, where the topmost sequents are initial sequents, and if a
  65. sequent stands below one or two other sequents, it must follow
  66. correctly by a rule of inference. The rules for $\Log{LK}$ are
  67. divided into two main types: \emph{logical} rules and
  68. \emph{structural} rules. The logical rules are named for the !!{main
  69. operator} of the !!{sentence} containing $!A$ and/or $!B$ in the
  70. lower sequent. Each one comes in two versions, one for inferring a
  71. sequent with the !!{sentence} containing the !!{operator} on the left,
  72. and one with the !!{sentence} on the right.

  19. rule that allows us to move !!{sentence}s to the right position. It's
  20. also important sometimes to be able to combine two identical
  21. !!{sentence}s into one, and to add !!a{sentence} on either side.

  15. \begin{explain}
  16. We've said what an initial sequent looks like, and we've given the
  17. rules of inference. !!^{derivation}s in the sequent calculus are
  18. inductively generated from these: each !!{derivation} either is an
  19. initial sequent on its own, or consists of one or two !!{derivation}s
  20. followed by an inference.
  21. \end{explain}
  23. \begin{defn}[$\Log{LK}$ !!{derivation}]
  24. An \emph{$\Log{LK}$-!!{derivation}} of a sequent~$S$ is a finite tree
  25. of sequents satisfying the following conditions:
  26. \begin{enumerate}
  27. \item The topmost sequents of the tree are initial sequents.
  28. \item The bottommost sequent of the tree is~$S$.
  29. \item Every sequent in the tree except $S$ is a premise of a correct
  30. application of an inference rule whose conclusion stands directly
  31. below that sequent in the tree.
  32. \end{enumerate}
  33. We then say that $S$ is the \emph{end-sequent} of the !!{derivation} and
  34. that $S$ is \emph{!!{derivable} in $\Log{LK}$} (or $\Log{LK}$-!!{derivable}).
  35. \end{defn}
  37. \begin{ex}
  38. Every initial sequent, e.g., $!C \Sequent !C$ is !!a{derivation}. We
  39. can obtain a new !!{derivation} from this by applying, say, the
  40. $\LeftR{\Weakening}$ rule,
  41. \begin{prooftree}
  42. \Axiom$ \Gamma \fCenter \Delta $
  43. \RightLabel{\LeftR{\Weakening}}
  44. \UnaryInf$ !A, \Gamma \fCenter \Delta$
  45. \end{prooftree}
  46. The rule, however, is meant to be general: we can replace the $!A$ in
  47. the rule with any !!{sentence}, e.g., also with~$!D$. If the premise
  48. matches our initial sequent $!C \Sequent !C$, that means that both
  49. $\Gamma$ and $\Delta$ are just~$!C$, and the conclusion would then be
  50. $!D, !C \Sequent !C$. So, the following is !!a{derivation}:
  51. \begin{prooftree}
  52. \Axiom$ !C \fCenter !C $
  53. \RightLabel{\LeftR{\Weakening}}
  54. \UnaryInf$ !D, !C \fCenter !C$
  55. \end{prooftree}
  56. We can now apply another rule, say $\LeftR{\Exchange}$, which allows
  57. us to switch two !!{sentence}s on the left. So, the following is also
  58. a correct !!{derivation}:
  59. \begin{prooftree}
  60. \Axiom$ !C \fCenter !C $
  61. \RightLabel{\LeftR{\Weakening}}
  62. \UnaryInf$ !D, !C \fCenter !C$
  63. \RightLabel{\LeftR{\Exchange}}
  64. \UnaryInf$ !C, !D \fCenter !C$
  65. \end{prooftree}
  66. In this application of the rule, which was given as
  67. \begin{prooftree}
  68. \Axiom$ \Gamma, !A, !B, \Pi \fCenter \Delta $
  69. \RightLabel{\LeftR{\Exchange}}
  70. \UnaryInf$ \Gamma, !B, !A, \Pi \fCenter \Delta,$
  71. \end{prooftree}
  72. both $\Gamma$ and $\Pi$ were empty, $\Delta$ is $!C$, and the roles of
  73. $!A$ and $!B$ are played by $!D$ and~$!C$, respectively. In much the
  74. same way, we also see that
  75. \begin{prooftree}
  76. \Axiom$ !D \fCenter !D $
  77. \RightLabel{\LeftR{\Weakening}}
  78. \UnaryInf$ !C, !D \fCenter !D$
  79. \end{prooftree}
  80. is !!a{derivation}. Now we can take these two !!{derivation}s, and combine
  81. them using $\RightR{\land}$. That rule was
  82. \begin{prooftree}
  83. \Axiom$\Gamma \fCenter \Delta, !A$
  84. \Axiom$ \Gamma \fCenter \Delta, !B$
  85. \RightLabel{\RightR{\land}}
  86. \BinaryInf$ \Gamma \fCenter \Delta, !A \land !B $
  87. \end{prooftree}
  88. In our case, the premises must match the last sequents of the
  89. !!{derivation}s ending in the premises. That means that $\Gamma$ is
  90. $!C, !D$, $\Delta$ is empty, $!A$ is $!C$ and $!B$ is $!D$. So the
  91. conclusion, if the inference should be correct, is $!C, !D \Sequent !C
  92. \land !D$.
  93. \begin{prooftree}
  94. \Axiom$ !C \fCenter !C $
  95. \RightLabel{\LeftR{\Weakening}}
  96. \UnaryInf$ !D, !C \fCenter !C$
  97. \RightLabel{\LeftR{\Exchange}}
  98. \UnaryInf$ !C, !D \fCenter !C$
  99. \Axiom$ !D \fCenter !D $
  100. \RightLabel{\LeftR{\Weakening}}
  101. \UnaryInf$ !C, !D \fCenter !D$
  102. \RightLabel{\RightR{\land}}
  103. \BinaryInf$ !C, !D \fCenter !C \land !D $
  104. \end{prooftree}
  105. Of course, we can also reverse the premises, then $!A$
  106. would be $!D$ and $!B$ would be~$!C$.
  107. \begin{prooftree}
  108. \Axiom$ !D \fCenter !D $
  109. \RightLabel{\LeftR{\Weakening}}
  110. \UnaryInf$ !C, !D \fCenter !D$
  111. \Axiom$ !C \fCenter !C $

  20. \begin{explain}
  21. Just as we've defined a number of important semantic notions
  22. (validity, entailment, satisfiability), we now define corresponding
  23. \emph{proof-theoretic notions}. These are not defined by appeal to
  24. satisfaction of !!{sentence}s in !!{structure}s, but by appeal to the
  25. !!{derivability} or !!{nonderivability} of certain sequents. It was
  26. an important discovery that these notions coincide. That they do is
  27. the content of the \emph{soundness} and \emph{completeness theorem}.
  28. \end{explain}
  30. \begin{defn}[Theorems]
  31. !!^a{sentence}~$!A$ is a \emph{theorem} if there is !!a{derivation}
  32. in~$\Log{LK}$ of the sequent $\quad \Sequent !A$. We write $\Proves
  33. !A$ if $!A$ is a theorem and $\Proves/ !A$ if it is not.
  34. \end{defn}
  36. \begin{defn}[!!^{derivability}]
  37. !!^a{sentence}~$!A$ is \emph{!!{derivable} from} a set of
  38. !!{sentence}s~$\Gamma$, $\Gamma \Proves !A$, iff there is a
  39. finite subset~$\Gamma_0 \subseteq \Gamma$ and a sequence $\Gamma_0'$
  40. of the !!{sentence}s in~$\Gamma_0$ such that $\Log{LK}$ !!{derive}s
  41. $\Gamma_0' \Sequent !A$. If $!A$ is not !!{derivable} from $\Gamma$
  42. we write $\Gamma \Proves/ !A$.
  43. \end{defn}
  45. Because of the contraction, weakening, and exchange rules, the order
  46. and number of !!{sentence}s in~$\Gamma_0'$ does not matter: if a
  47. sequent $\Gamma_0' \Sequent !A$ is !!{derivable}, then so is
  48. $\Gamma_0'' \Sequent !A$ for any $\Gamma_0''$ that contains the same
  49. !!{sentence}s as~$\Gamma_0'$. For instance, if $\Gamma_0 = \{!B, !C\}$
  50. then both $\Gamma_0' = \tuple{!B, !B, !C}$ and $\Gamma_0'' =
  51. \tuple{!C, !C, !B}$ are sequences containing just the !!{sentence}s
  52. in~$\Gamma_0$. If a sequent containing one is !!{derivable}, so is the
  53. other, e.g.:
  54. \begin{prooftree}
  55. \AxiomC{}
  56. \Deduce$!B, !B, !C \fCenter !A$
  57. \RightLabel{\LeftR{\Contraction}}
  58. \UnaryInf$!B, !C \fCenter !A$
  59. \RightLabel{\LeftR{\Exchange}}
  60. \UnaryInf$!C, !B \fCenter !A$
  61. \RightLabel{\LeftR{\Weakening}}
  62. \UnaryInf$!C, !C, !B \fCenter !A$
  63. \end{prooftree}

  65. !!{sentence}s then $\Gamma_0 \Sequent !A$ is any sequent where the
  66. antecedent is a sequence of !!{sentence}s in~$\Gamma_0$ and tacitly include
  67. contractions, exchanges, and weakenings if necessary.

  221. \olref[syn][sem]{prop:quant-terms}, if
  222. $\Sat{M}{\lforall[x][!A(x)]}$, then $\Sat{M}{!A(t)}$. Since
  223. $\Sat/{M}{!A(t)}$, $\Sat/{M}{\lforall[x][!A(x)]}$ . In case (b) and
  224. (c), $\Struct{M}$ also satisfies $\lforall[x][!A(x)], \Gamma
  225. \Sequent \Delta$. Since $\Struct M$ was arbitrary,
  226. $\lforall[x][!A(x)], \Gamma \Sequent \Delta$ is valid.
  227. \item The last inference is \RightR{\lexists}: Exercise.
  228. \item The last inference is \RightR{\lforall}: Then there is
  229. !!a{formula}~$!A(x)$ and !!a{constant}~$a$ such that $\pi$ ends in
  230. \begin{prooftree}
  231. \AxiomC{}
  232. \Deduce$\Gamma \fCenter \Delta, !A(a)$
  233. \RightLabel{\RightR{\lforall}}
  234. \UnaryInf$\Gamma \fCenter \Delta, \lforall[x][!A(x)]$
  235. \end{prooftree}
  236. where the eigenvariable condition is satisfied, i.e., $a$ does not
  237. occur in $!A(x)$, $\Gamma$, or $\Delta$. By induction hypothesis,
  238. the premise of the last inference is valid. We have to show that
  239. the conclusion is valid as well, i.e., that for any
  240. !!{structure}~$\Struct M$, (a) $\Sat{M}{\lforall[x][!A(x)]}$, (b)
  241. $\Sat/{M}{!C}$ for some $!C \in \Gamma$, or (c)~$\Sat{M}{!C}$ for
  242. some $!C \in \Delta$.
  244. Suppose $\Struct{M}$ is an arbitrary !!{structure}. If (b) or (c)
  245. holds, we are done, so suppose neither holds: for all $!C \in
  246. \Gamma$, $\Sat{M}{!C}$, and for all $!C \in \Delta$,
  247. $\Sat/{M}{!C}$. We have to show that (a) holds, i.e.,
  248. $\Sat{M}{\lforall[x][!A(x)]}$. By
  249. \olref[syn][ass]{prop:sat-quant}, if suffices to show that
  250. $\Sat{M}{!A(x)}[s]$ for all variable assignments~$s$. So let $s$ be
  251. an arbitrary variable assignment. Consider the
  252. structure~$\Struct{M'}$ which is just like~$\Struct{M}$ except
  253. $\Assign{a}{M'} = s(x)$. By
  254. \olref[syn][ext]{cor:extensionality-sent}, for any $!C \in \Gamma$,
  255. $\Sat{M'}{!C}$ since $a$ does not occur in~$\Gamma$, and for any
  256. $!C \in \Delta$, $\Sat/{M'}{!C}$. But the premise is valid, so
  257. $\Sat{M'}{!A(a)}$. By \olref[syn][ass]{prop:sentence-sat-true},
  258. $\Sat{M'}{!A(a)}[s]$, since $!A(a)$ is a sentence. Now $\varAssign{s}{s}{x}$
  259. with $s(x) = \Value{a}{M'}[s]$, since we've defined $\Struct{M'}$ in
  260. just this way. So \olref[syn][ext]{prop:ext-formulas} applies, and
  261. we get $\Sat{M'}{!A(x)}[s]$. Since $a$ does not occur in~$!A(x)$, by
  262. \olref[syn][ext]{prop:extensionality}, $\Sat{M}{!A(x)}[s]$. Since
  263. $s$ was arbitrary, we've completed the proof that
  264. $\Sat{M}{!A(x)}[s]$ for all variable assignments.
  265. \item The last inference is \LeftR{\lexists}: Exercise.
  266. }{}
  267. \end{enumerate}
  268. Now let's consider the possible inferences with two premises.
  269. \begin{enumerate}
  270. \item The last inference is a cut: then $\pi$ ends in
  271. \begin{prooftree}
  272. \AxiomC{}
  273. \Deduce$\Gamma \fCenter \Delta, !A$
  274. \AxiomC{}
  275. \Deduce$!A, \Pi \fCenter \Lambda$
  276. \RightLabel{\Cut}
  277. \BinaryInf$\Gamma, \Pi \fCenter \Delta, \Lambda$
  278. \end{prooftree}
  279. Let \iftag{FOL}{$\Struct{M}$ be !!a{structure}}{$\pAssign{v}$ be
  280. !!a{valuation}}. By induction hypothesis, the premises are valid,
  281. so \iftag{FOL}{$\Struct{M}$}{$\pAssign{v}$} satisfies both premises.
  282. We distinguish two cases: (a) $\iftag{FOL}{\Sat/{M}}{\pSat/{v}}{!A}$
  283. and (b) $\iftag{FOL}{\Sat{M}}{\pSat{v}}{!A}$. In case (a), in order
  284. for \iftag{FOL}{$\Struct{M}$}{$\pAssign{v}$} to satisfy the left
  285. premise, it must satisfy $\Gamma \Sequent \Delta$. But then it also
  286. satisfies the conclusion. In case (b), in order for
  287. \iftag{FOL}{$\Struct{M}$}{$\pAssign{v}$} to satisfy the right
  288. premise, it must satisfy $\Pi \setminus \Lambda$. Again,
  289. \iftag{FOL}{$\Struct{M}$}{$\pAssign{v}$} satisfies the conclusion.
  290. \item The last inference is \RightR{\land}. Then $\pi$ ends in
  291. \begin{prooftree}
  292. \AxiomC{}
  293. \Deduce$\Gamma \fCenter \Delta, !A$
  294. \AxiomC{}
  295. \Deduce$\Gamma \fCenter \Delta, !B$
  296. \RightLabel{\RightR{\land}}
  297. \BinaryInf$\Gamma \fCenter \Delta, !A \land !B$
  298. \end{prooftree}
  299. Consider \iftag{FOL}{!!a{structure}~$\Struct
  300. M$}{!!a{valuation}~$\pAssign{v}$}. If
  301. \iftag{FOL}{$\Struct{M}$}{$\pAssign{v}$} satisfies $\Gamma \Sequent
  302. \Delta$, we are done. So suppose it doesn't. Since $\Gamma \fCenter
  303. \Delta, !A$ is valid by induction hypothesis,
  304. $\iftag{FOL}{\Sat{M}}{\pSat{v}}{!A}$. Similarly, since $\Gamma
  305. \Sequent \Delta, !B$ is valid,
  306. $\iftag{FOL}{\Sat{M}}{\pSat{v}}{!B}$. But then
  307. $\iftag{FOL}{\Sat{M}}{\pSat{v}}{!A \land !B}$.
  308. \item The last inference is \LeftR{\lor}: Exercise.
  309. \item The last inference is \LeftR{\lif}. Then $\pi$ ends in
  310. \begin{prooftree}
  311. \AxiomC{}
  312. \Deduce$\Gamma \fCenter \Delta, !A$
  313. \AxiomC{}
  314. \Deduce$!B, \Pi \fCenter \Lambda$
  315. \RightLabel{\LeftR{\lif}}
  316. \BinaryInf$!A \lif !B, \Gamma, \Pi \fCenter \Delta, \Lambda$
  317. \end{prooftree}
  318. Again, consider
  319. \iftag{FOL}{!!a{structure}~$\Struct{M}$}{!!a{valuation}~$\pAssign{v}$}
  320. and suppose \iftag{FOL}{$\Struct{M}$}{$\pAssign{v}$} doesn't satisfy
  321. $\Gamma, \Pi \Sequent \Delta, \Lambda$. We have to show that
  322. $\iftag{FOL}{\Sat/{M}}{\pSat/{v}}{!A \lif !B}$. If
  323. \iftag{FOL}{$\Struct{M}$}{$\pAssign{v}$} doesn't satisfy $\Gamma,
  324. \Pi \Sequent \Delta, \Lambda$, it satisfies neither $\Gamma \Sequent
  325. \Delta$ nor $\Pi \Sequent \Lambda$. Since, $\Gamma \Sequent \Delta,
  326. !A$ is valid, we have $\iftag{FOL}{\Sat{M}}{\pSat{v}}{!A}$. Since
  327. $!B, \Pi \Sequent \Lambda$ is valid, we have
  328. $\iftag{FOL}{\Sat/{M}}{\pSat/{v}}{!B}$. But then
  329. $\iftag{FOL}{\Sat/{M}}{\pSat/{v}}{!A \lif !B}$, which is what we
  330. wanted to show.
  331. \end{enumerate}
  332. \end{proof}

  346. \begin{cor}
  347. \ollabel{cor:weak-soundness}
  348. If $\Proves !A$ then $!A$ is \iftag{FOL}{valid}{a tautology}.
  349. \end{cor}
  351. \begin{cor}
  352. \ollabel{cor:entailment-soundness}
  353. If $\Gamma \Proves !A$ then $\Gamma \Entails !A$.
  354. \end{cor}
  356. \begin{proof}
  357. If $\Gamma \Proves !A$ then for some finite subset $\Gamma_0 \subseteq
  358. \Gamma$, there is !!a{derivation} of $\Gamma_0 \Sequent !A$. By
  359. \olref{thm:sequent-soundness}, every
  360. \iftag{FOL}{!!{structure}~$\Struct{M}$}{!!{valuation}~$\pAssign{v}$}
  361. either makes some $!B \in \Gamma_0$ false or makes $!A$ true. Hence,
  362. if $\iftag{FOL}{\Sat{M}}{\pSat{v}}{\Gamma}$ then also
  363. $\iftag{FOL}{\Sat{M}}{\pSat{v}}{!A}$.
  364. \end{proof}
  366. \begin{cor}
  367. \ollabel{cor:consistency-soundness}
  368. If $\Gamma$ is satisfiable, then it is consistent.
  369. \end{cor}
  371. \begin{proof}
  372. We prove the contrapositive. Suppose that $\Gamma$ is not consistent.
  373. Then there is a finite $\Gamma_0 \subseteq \Gamma$ and !!a{derivation}
  374. of $\Gamma_0 \Sequent \quad$. By \olref{thm:sequent-soundness},
  375. $\Gamma_0 \Sequent \quad$ is valid. In other words, for every
  376. \iftag{FOL}{!!{structure}~$\Struct{M}$}{!!{valuation}~$\pAssign{v}$},
  377. there is $!C \in \Gamma_0$ so that
  378. $\iftag{FOL}{\Sat/{M}}{\pSat/{v}}{!C}$, and since $\Gamma_0
  379. \subseteq \Gamma$, that $!C$ is also in~$\Gamma$. Thus, no
  380. \iftag{FOL}{$\Struct{M}$}{$\pAssign{v}$} satisfies~$\Gamma$, and
  381. $\Gamma$ is not satisfiable.
  382. \end{proof}
