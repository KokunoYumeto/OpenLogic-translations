  11. \olsection{Formation Sequences}
  13. Defining !!{formula}s via an inductive definition, and the
  14. complementary technique of proving properties of !!{formula}s via
  15. induction, is an elegant and efficient approach. However, it can
  16. also be useful to consider a more bottom-up, step-by-step approach
  17. to the construction of !!{formula}s, which we do here using the
  18. notion of a \emph{formation sequence}.
  20. \begin{defn}[Formation sequences for formulas]
  21. \ollabel{defn:fseq-frm}
  22. A finite sequence $\tuple{!A_0,\dotsc,!A_n}$ of strings of
  23. symbols from the language~$\Lang L_0$ is a \emph{formation
  24. sequence} for $!A$ if $!A \ident !A_n$ and for all $i \leq n$,
  25. either $!A_i$ is an atomic formula or there exist $j,k < i$
  26. such that one of the following holds:
  27. \begin{enumerate}
  28. \tagitem{prvNot}{$!A_i \ident \lnot !A_j$.}{}%
  29. \tagitem{prvAnd}{$!A_i \ident (!A_j \land !A_k)$.}{}%
  30. \tagitem{prvOr}{$!A_i \ident (!A_j \lor !A_k)$.}{}%
  31. \tagitem{prvIf}{$!A_i \ident (!A_j \lif !A_k)$.}{}%
  32. \tagitem{prvIff}{$!A_i \ident (!A_j \liff !A_k)$.}{}%
  33. \end{enumerate}
  34. \end{defn}
  36. \begin{ex}
  37. \[
  38. \tuple{
  39. \Obj p_0,
  40. \Obj p_1,
  41. (\Obj p_1 \land \Obj p_0),
  42. \lnot (\Obj p_1 \land \Obj p_0)
  43. }
  44. \]
  45. is a formation sequence of
  46. $\lnot (\Obj p_1 \land \Obj p_0)$, as is
  47. \[
  48. \tuple{
  49. \Obj p_0,
  50. \Obj p_1,
  51. \Obj p_0,
  52. (\Obj p_1 \land \Obj p_0),
  53. (\Obj p_0 \lif \Obj p_1),
  54. \lnot (\Obj p_1 \land \Obj p_0)
  55. }.
  56. \]
  57. %
  58. As can be seen from the second example, formation sequences
  59. may contain `junk': formulas which are redundant or do not
  60. contribute to the construction.
  61. \end{ex}
  63. \begin{prop}
  64. \ollabel{prop:formed}
  65. Every !!{formula}~$!A$ in~$\Frm[L_0]$ has a formation sequence.
  66. \end{prop}
  68. \begin{proof}
  69. Suppose $!A$ is atomic. Then the sequence~$\tuple{!A}$ is a
  70. formation sequence for~$!A$.
  71. %
  72. Now suppose that $!B$ and~$!C$ have formation sequences
  73. $\tuple{!B_0,\dotsc,!B_n}$ and $\tuple{!C_0,\dotsc,!C_m}$
  74. respectively.
  75. %
  76. \begin{enumerate}
  77. \tagitem{prvNot}{If $!A \ident \lnot !B$,
  78. then $\tuple{!B_0,\dotsc,!B_n,\lnot !B_n}$
  79. is a formation sequence for~$!A$.}{}
  80. \tagitem{prvAnd}{If $!A \ident (!B \land !C)$,
  81. then $\tuple{!B_0,\dotsc,!B_n,!C_0,\dotsc,!C_m,(!B_n \land !C_m)}$
  82. is a formation sequence for~$!A$.}{}
  83. \tagitem{prvOr}{If $!A \ident (!B \lor !C)$,
  84. then $\tuple{!B_0,\dotsc,!B_n,!C_0,\dotsc,!C_m,(!B_n \lor !C_m)}$
  85. is a formation sequence for~$!A$.}{}
  86. \tagitem{prvIf}{If $!A \ident (!B \lif !C)$,
  87. then $\tuple{!B_0,\dotsc,!B_n,!C_0,\dotsc,!C_m,(!B_n \lif !C_m)}$
  88. is a formation sequence for~$!A$.}{}
  89. \tagitem{prvIff}{If $!A \ident (!B \liff !C)$,
  90. then $\tuple{!B_0,\dotsc,!B_n,!C_0,\dotsc,!C_m,(!B_n \liff !C_m)}$
  91. is a formation sequence for~$!A$.}{}
  92. \end{enumerate}
  93. By the principle of induction on !!{formula}s,
  94. every !!{formula} has a formation sequence.
  95. \end{proof}
  97. We can also prove the converse. This is important because it shows
  98. that our two ways of defining formulas are equivalent: they give
  99. the same results. It also means that we can prove theorems about
  100. formulas by using ordinary induction on the length of formation
  101. sequences.
  103. \begin{lem}
  104. \ollabel{lem:fseq-init}
  105. Suppose that $\tuple{!A_0,\dotsc,!A_n}$ is a formation sequence
  106. for~$!A_n$, and that $k \leq n$. Then $\tuple{!A_0,\dotsc,!A_k}$
  107. is a formation sequence for~$!A_k$.
  108. \end{lem}
  110. \begin{proof}
  111. Exercise.
  112. \end{proof}
  114. \begin{thm}
  115. \ollabel{thm:fseq-frm-equiv}
  116. $\Frm[L_0]$ is the set of all strings of symbols
  117. in the language~$\Lang L_0$ with a formation sequence.
  118. \end{thm}
  120. \begin{proof}
  121. Let $F$ be the set of all strings of symbols in the
  122. language~$\Lang L_0$ that have a formation sequence.
  123. We have seen in \olref[pl][syn][fseq]{prop:formed} that
  124. $\Frm[L_0] \subseteq F$, so now we prove the converse.
  126. Suppose $!A$ has a formation sequence $\tuple{!A_0,\dotsc,!A_n}$.
  127. We prove that $!A \in \Frm[L_0]$ by strong induction on~$n$.
  128. Our induction hypothesis is that every string of symbols with a
  129. formation sequence of length $m < n$ is in~$\Frm[L_0]$.
  130. By the definition of a formation sequence, either $!A_n$ is
  131. atomic or there must exist $j,k < n$ such that one of the
  132. following is the case:
  133. \begin{enumerate}
  134. \tagitem{prvNot}{$!A_n \ident \lnot !A_j$.}{}%
  135. \tagitem{prvAnd}{$!A_n \ident (!A_j \land !A_k)$.}{}%
  136. \tagitem{prvOr}{$!A_n \ident (!A_j \lor !A_k)$.}{}%
  137. \tagitem{prvIf}{$!A_n \ident (!A_j \lif !A_k)$.}{}%
  138. \tagitem{prvIff}{$!A_n \ident (!A_j \liff !A_k)$.}{}%
  139. \end{enumerate}
  140. Now we reason by cases. If $!A_n$ is atomic then
  141. $!A_n \in \Frm[L_0]$. Suppose instead that $!A \equiv
  142. (!A_j \land !A_k)$. By \olref[pl][syn][fseq]{lem:fseq-init},
  143. $\tuple{!A_0,\dotsc,!A_j}$ and $\tuple{!A_0,\dotsc,!A_k}$ are
  144. formation sequences for $!A_j$ and~$!A_k$ respectively. Since
  145. these are proper initial subsequences of the formation sequence
  146. for~$!A$, they both have length less than~$n$. Therefore by
  147. the induction hypothesis, $!A_j$ and~$!A_k$ are in~$\Frm[L_0]$,
  148. and so by the definition of !!a{formula}, so is
  149. $(!A_j \land !A_k)$. The other cases follow by parallel
  150. reasoning.
  151. \end{proof}

  78. \begin{defn}[Formula]
  79. \ollabel{defn:formulas}
  80. The set~$\Frm[L_0]$ of \emph{!!{formula}s} of propositional logic
  81. is defined inductively as follows:
  82. \begin{enumerate}
  83. \tagitem{prvFalse}{$\lfalse$ is an atomic !!{formula}.}{}
  85. \tagitem{prvTrue}{$\ltrue$ is an atomic !!{formula}.}{}
  87. \item Every !!{propositional variable}~$\Obj p_i$ is an atomic
  88. !!{formula}.
  90. \tagitem{prvNot}{If $!A$ is !!a{formula}, then $\lnot !A$ is
  91. !!a{formula}.}{}
  93. \tagitem{prvAnd}{If $!A$ and $!B$ are !!{formula}s, then $(!A \land
  94. !B)$ is !!a{formula}.}{}
  96. \tagitem{prvOr}{If $!A$ and $!B$ are !!{formula}s, then $(!A \lor !B)$
  97. is !!a{formula}.}{}
  99. \tagitem{prvIf}{If $!A$ and $!B$ are !!{formula}s, then $(!A \lif !B)$
  100. is !!a{formula}.}{}
  102. \tagitem{prvIff}{If $!A$ and $!B$ are !!{formula}s, then $(!A \liff !B)$
  103. is !!a{formula}.}{}
  105. \tagitem{limitClause}{Nothing else is !!a{formula}.}{}
  106. \end{enumerate}
  107. \end{defn}
  109. \begin{explain}
  110. The definition of !!{formula}s is an
  111. \emph{inductive definition}. Essentially, we construct the set of
  112. !!{formula}s in infinitely many stages. In the initial stage, we
  113. pronounce all atomic formulas to be formulas; this corresponds to the
  114. first few cases of the definition, i.e., the cases for
  115. \iftag{prvTrue}{$\ltrue$, }{}%
  116. \iftag{prvFalse}{$\lfalse$, }{}%
  117. $\Obj p_i$. ``Atomic !!{formula}''
  118. thus means any !!{formula} of this form.
  120. The other cases of the definition give rules for constructing new
  121. !!{formula}s out of !!{formula}s already constructed. At the second
  122. stage, we can use them to construct !!{formula}s out of atomic
  123. !!{formula}s. At the third stage, we construct new formulas from the
  124. atomic formulas and those obtained in the second stage, and so on. A
  125. !!{formula} is anything that is eventually constructed at such a
  126. stage, and nothing else.
  127. \end{explain}
  129. When writing a formula $(!B \ast !C)$ constructed from $!B$, $!C$
  130. using a two-place connective~$\ast$, we will often leave out the
  131. outermost pair of parentheses and write simply~$!B \ast !C$.

  11. \olsection{Introduction}
  13. Propositional logic deals with !!{formula}s that are built from
  14. !!{propositional variable}s using the propositional connectives
  15. $\lnot$, $\land$, $\lor$, $\lif$, and $\liff$. Intuitively,
  16. !!a{propositional variable}~$p$ stands for a sentence or proposition
  17. that is true or false. Whenever the ``truth value'' of the
  18. !!{propositional variable} in !!a{formula} is determined, so is the
  19. truth value of any !!{formula}s formed from them using propositional
  20. connectives. We say that propositional logic is \emph{truth
  21. functional}, because its semantics is given by functions of truth
  22. values. In particular, in propositional logic we leave out of
  23. consideration any further determination of truth and falsity, e.g.,
  24. whether something is necessarily true rather than just contingently
  25. true, or whether something is known to be true, or whether something
  26. is true now rather than was true or will be true. We only consider
  27. two truth values true ($\True$) and false ($\False$), and so exclude
  28. from discussion the possibility that a statement may be neither true
  29. nor false, or only half true. We also concentrate only on connectives where
  30. the truth value of !!a{formula} built from them is completely
  31. determined by the truth values of its parts (and not, say, on its
  32. meaning). In particular, whether the truth value of conditionals in
  33. English is truth functional in this sense is contentious. The material
  34. conditional~$\lif$ is; other logics deal with conditionals that are
  35. not truth functional.
  37. In order to develop the theory and metatheory of truth-functional
  38. propositional logic, we must first define the syntax and semantics of
  39. its expressions. We will describe one way of constructing
  40. !!{formula}s from !!{propositional variable}s using the connectives.
  41. Alternative definitions are possible. Other systems will choose
  42. different symbols, will select different sets of connectives as
  43. primitive, and will use parentheses differently (or even not at all,
  44. as in the case of so-called Polish notation). What all approaches
  45. have in common, though, is that the formation rules define the set of
  46. !!{formula}s \emph{inductively}. If done properly, every expression
  47. can result essentially in only one way according to the formation
  48. rules. The inductive definition resulting in expressions that are
  49. \emph{uniquely readable} means we can give meanings to these
  50. expressions using the same method---inductive definition.
  52. Giving the meaning of expressions is the domain of semantics. The
  53. central concept in semantics for propositional logic is that of
  54. satisfaction in !!a{valuation}. !!^a{valuation}~$\pAssign{v}$ assigns
  55. truth values $\True$, $\False$ to the !!{propositional variable}s. Any
  56. !!{valuation} determines a truth value $\pValue{v}(!A)$ for any
  57. !!{formula}~$!A$. !!^a{formula} is satisfied in
  58. !!a{valuation}~$\pAssign{v}$ iff $\pValue{v}(!A) = \True$---we write
  59. this as $\pSat{v}{!A}$. This relation can also be defined by induction on
  60. the structure of~$!A$, using the truth functions for the logical
  61. connectives to define, say, satisfaction of $!A \land !B$ in terms of
  62. satisfaction (or not) of $!A$ and~$!B$.
  64. On the basis of the satisfaction relation $\pSat{v}{!A}$ for sentences
  65. we can then define the basic semantic notions of tautology,
  66. entailment, and satisfiability. !!^a{formula} is a tautology,
  67. $\Entails !A$, if every !!{valuation} satisfies it, i.e.,
  68. $\pValue{v}(!A) = \True$ for any~$\pAssign{v}$. It is entailed by a
  69. set of !!{formula}s, $\Gamma \Entails !A$, if every !!{valuation} that
  70. satisfies all the !!{formula}s in~$\Gamma$ also satisfies~$!A$. And a
  71. set of !!{formula}s is satisfiable if some !!{valuation} satisfies all
  72. !!{formula}s in it at the same time. Because !!{formula}s are
  73. inductively defined, and satisfaction is in turn defined by induction
  74. on the structure of !!{formula}s, we can use induction to prove
  75. properties of our semantics and to relate the semantic notions
  76. defined.

  14. \ollabel{thm:induction}
  15. If some property~$P$ holds for all the atomic !!{formula}s and is
  16. such that
  17. \begin{enumerate}
  18. \tagitem{prvNot}{it holds for $\lnot !A$ whenever it holds
  19. for~$!A$;}{}
  20. \tagitem{prvAnd}{it holds for $(!A \land !B)$
  21. whenever it holds for $!A$ and~$!B$;}{}
  22. \tagitem{prvOr}{it holds for $(!A \lor !B)$

  24. \tagitem{prvIf}{it holds for $(!A \lif !B)$

  26. \tagitem{prvIff}{it holds for $(!A \liff !B)$

  28. \end{enumerate}
