Chapter. Second-order Logic and Set Theory. Editorial note. This section deals with coding powersets and the continuum in second-order logic. Use with caution and report anything that's false or unclear. End editorial note.

Section. Introduction. Since second-order logic can quantify over subsets of the domain as well as functions, it is to be expected that some amount, at least, of set theory can be carried out in second-order logic. By “carry out,” we mean that it is possible to express set theoretic properties and statements in second-order logic, and is possible without any special, non-logical vocabulary for sets (for example, the membership predicate symbol of set theory). For instance, we can define unions and intersections of sets and the subset relationship, but also compare the sizes of sets, and state results such as Cantor's Theorem. End section.

We know from Cantor's Theorem that there are non-enumerable sets, and in fact, that there are infinitely many different levels of infinite sizes. Set theory develops an entire arithmetic of sizes of sets, and assigns infinite cardinal numbers to sets. The natural numbers serve as the cardinal numbers measuring the sizes of finite sets. The cardinality of denumerable sets is the first infinite cardinality, called aleph zero (“aleph-nought” or “aleph-zero”). The next infinite size is aleph one . It is the smallest size a set can be without being countable (that is, of size aleph zero ). We can define “ capital X has size aleph zero ” as Aleph zero of capital X if and only if both Inf of capital X and Count of capital X . capital X has size aleph one if and only if all its subsets are finite or have size aleph zero , but is not itself of size aleph zero . Hence we can express this by the formula Aleph one of capital X abbreviates the conjunction of two conditions. For every subset capital Y of the domain, if capital Y is a subset of capital X, then either not Inf of capital Y or Aleph zero of capital Y. And not Aleph zero of capital X . Being of size aleph two is defined similarly, etc. Source anomaly note. Source caveat. The proposed Aleph one condition includes capital X among its own subsets. It is the size of the power set of the natural numbers , or, equivalently, the size of the real numbers . That a set is the size of the continuum can also be expressed in second-order logic, but requires a bit more work. End section.

Section. The Power of the Continuum. In second-order logic we can quantify over subsets of the domain, but not over sets of subsets of the domain. To do this directly, we would need third-order logic. For instance, if we wanted to state Cantor's Theorem that there is no injective function from the power set of a set to the set itself, we might try to formulate it as “for every set capital X , and every set capital P , if capital P is the power set of capital X , then not capital P has cardinality at most that of capital X ”. And to say that capital P is the power set of capital X would require formalizing that the elements of capital P are all and only the subsets of capital X , so something like for every capital Y, capital P applied to capital Y if and only if capital Y is a subset of capital X . The problem lies in capital P applied to capital Y : that is not a formula of second-order logic, since only terms can be arguments to one-place relation variables like capital P . We can, however, simulate quantification over sets of sets, if the domain is large enough. The idea is to make use of the fact that two-place relations capital R relate elements of the domain to elements of the domain. Given such an capital R , we can collect all the elements to which some x is capital R -related: the set of elements y of the domain of M such that capital R relates x to y is the set “coded by” x . Conversely, if capital Z, a subset of the power set of the domain of M, is some collection of subsets of the domain of M , and there are at least as many elements of the domain of M as there are sets in capital Z , then there is also a relation capital R, a subset of the Cartesian square of the domain of M, such that every capital Y in capital Z is coded by some x using capital R .
